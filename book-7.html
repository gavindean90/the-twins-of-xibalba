Book 7 of VII

The Ascension

Beneath the mountains, cold and deep, Where ancient kings no councils keep, Where roots through halls of darkness creep, And rivers through the silence sweep, Lie broken courts of Xibalba. No trumpet calls the lords to rise, No golden throne commands the skies, And silence fills the hollow eyes Of fallen kings in Xibalba.

The brothers walked the sunless plain, Where none had heard the summer rain, And sought the fathers who were slain, Whose bones beneath the earth remain Within the courts of Xibalba. No sword they bore, no shield of gold, But memories of the deeds of old, And promises their hearts would hold Beyond the death of Xibalba.

They came upon the ancient ground, Where once the rubber ball had bound Their fathers to the hollow sound Of death's deceit, and there they found The ruined court of Xibalba. The stones were cracked, the walls were bare, And dust lay thick upon the stair, But still the voice of grief hung there Among the halls of Xibalba.

Then Hunahpu knelt on the stone, Where once his father's blood had flown, And touched the dust with hands his own, And heard a voice, so faintly known, Beneath the courts of Xibalba. It whispered from the silent clay, Of fields beneath the light of day, And of the life that passed away Within the depths of Xibalba.

Then Xbalanque approached the place, And brushed the earth with solemn grace, Until beneath its darkened face They found the bones, and every trace Of those who died in Xibalba. The brothers gathered all they knew, And laid the ancient bones anew, And watched the pale light trembling through The darkness of Xibalba.

They called the names of those who fell, Whose sufferings no tongue could tell, And bade their spirits break the spell That held them in the sunless dell Beneath the stones of Xibalba. Then from the earth a figure rose, As maize beneath the summer grows, And life returned to one who knows The bitter sleep of Xibalba.

His limbs were clothed in tender green, His face was pale, his eyes serene, And in his hair the leaves were seen Of fields that once his home had been Above the courts of Xibalba. One Hunahpu stood tall and fair, With living blossoms in his hair, And drew the cold and heavy air That brooded over Xibalba.

The brothers stood with shining eyes, And heard their father's spirit rise, But something in his darkened eyes Still bore the shadow of the lies That brought him down to Xibalba. For though his voice returned again, And life was stirring in his vein, The ancient wound of death remained Within the courts of Xibalba.

Then Hunahpu approached his sire, And spoke with all his heart's desire, To call again the living fire, And lift his father from the mire Of endless night in Xibalba. “O father, speak, and name thy face, Thy mouth, thine eyes, their rightful place, That we may break the final trace Of death's deceit in Xibalba.”

The father spoke his mouth alone, And touched the features dimly known, But where his eyes had brightly shone, The shadow claimed them for its own Beneath the courts of Xibalba. No further word his lips could say, No further light could find its way, And something of the mortal clay Was lost forever in Xibalba.

Then Xbalanque bowed down his head, And looked upon the ancient dead, And thought of all the blood once shed, And all the paths their footsteps led Into the depths of Xibalba. For they had conquered every snare, And robbed the kings of throne and chair, Yet could not wholly summon there The lives once lost in Xibalba.

The brothers stood in silence long, And heard the river's distant song, And knew that though their arms were strong, Not every sorrow could be wronged Within the courts of Xibalba. For death had lost its cruel command, Yet still its mark was on the land, And none could wholly understand The mysteries of Xibalba.

Then Hunahpu arose once more, And spoke beside the ancient floor, Where fathers played in days before, And death had closed the iron door Against the sons of Xibalba. “Though not to earth thy feet return, Thy name shall live where fires burn, And every field of maize shall learn Thy triumph over Xibalba.”

“No king shall take thy name away, Nor bury it beneath the clay, For every seed that greets the day Shall speak of life that would not stay Forever bound in Xibalba. And when the harvest fields are bare, And winter chills the silent air, The spring shall find thy spirit there, Beyond the reach of Xibalba.”

Then from the ancient father came A blessing none could put to shame, And through the earth his whispered name Awoke the small and hidden flame Beneath the stones of Xibalba. The roots began to stir below, And through the cracks the maize did grow, Where neither sun nor wind could blow Upon the fields of Xibalba.

And Seven Hunahpu arose, From out the dust of long repose, And where the darkened river flows, The brothers laid the ancient woes To rest beneath Xibalba. They gave their fathers honored place, Where memory would not efface The courage of the mortal race That challenged death in Xibalba.

Then from the stones there came a sound, As though the heart beneath the ground Had struck a ball, whose echoes bound The vaulted caverns all around The silent courts of Xibalba. The brothers heard the distant beat, And felt the earth beneath their feet, As though the dead and living meet Beyond the halls of Xibalba.

The sound grew faint, the shadows fled, And peace descended on the dead, While far above the earth was spread A light no eye had witnessed yet Beyond the depths of Xibalba. The brothers turned toward the stair, And felt a stirring in the air, As though the heavens waited there Above the gates of Xibalba.

They climbed the paths their feet had known, Past rivers carved through ancient stone, Past broken gates and pillars thrown, And left the silent kings alone Within the halls of Xibalba. The black road whispered as before, But none would heed its promise more, And soon they reached the hidden door That opened from Xibalba.

They crossed where rivers ran with blood, And passed the dark and deadly flood, And climbed where ancient cedars stood, Their roots within the sacred mud That drank the streams of Xibalba. The mountain paths began to rise, And through the stone the brothers' eyes Beheld the pale and distant skies Beyond the walls of Xibalba.

The first cool wind upon them blew, And brought the scent of morning dew, And through the clouds the heavens grew From deepest black to tender blue Above the depths of Xibalba. They heard the voices of the trees, The rustling of the summer breeze, And distant birds among the leaves Beyond the reach of Xibalba.

The brothers paused upon the height, And looked upon the world of light, Where fields of maize, in colors bright, Were waking from the ancient night That brooded over Xibalba. No mortal voice proclaimed their fame, No herald called them forth by name, Yet all the earth was not the same As when they entered Xibalba.

For far away, within her door, Their grandmother upon the floor Beheld the canes she planted before, When both her grandsons went to war Against the kings of Xibalba. They once had withered in the clay, And seemed to waste their lives away, But now their leaves were green as May, And death had lost in Xibalba.

Old Xmucane beheld them grow, And felt a joy she scarce could know, For through the canes the life did flow Of those who walked the world below And conquered dark Xibalba. She touched the leaves with trembling hand, And watched them rise above the sand, And knew her children yet would stand Beyond the gates of Xibalba.

The brothers saw the world spread wide, The mountain ridge, the river's tide, And all the valleys side by side, Where living creatures still abide Unknowing of Xibalba. They saw the maize upon the plain, The fields that drank the falling rain, And heard the ancient world again Rejoice above Xibalba.

Yet neither took the homeward way, Nor sought the fields of green array, For something called them from the day, Beyond the mountains, far away From all the courts of Xibalba. The heavens trembled overhead, And light upon the mountains spread, As though the pathways of the dead Had opened over Xibalba.

Then Hunahpu beheld the sky, And watched the silent clouds drift by, And knew that he must rise on high, Beyond the reach of mortal eye And all the depths of Xibalba. His brother stood beside him there, And felt the stirring of the air, And knew that both were called to bear The light above Xibalba.

No wings arose upon their backs, No thunder broke the mountain tracks, But heaven opened through the black, And all the shadows hurried back Into the depths of Xibalba. The brothers rose above the plain, Above the fields of growing grain, Beyond the clouds that carried rain Across the roofs of Xibalba.

Then Hunahpu began to shine, His brow a crown of fire divine, And all the heavens seemed to twine Their golden radiance with his sign Above the depths of Xibalba. The mountains glowed beneath his ray, The rivers sparkled far away, And night surrendered to the day That conquered dark Xibalba.

The eastern sky was set aflame, And from the heights the morning came, And all creation knew his name, Though none could see the boy who came From out the courts of Xibalba. The sun arose in golden might, And poured upon the earth his light, And banished from the mortal sight The endless gloom of Xibalba.

Then Xbalanque arose beside, And crossed the heavens, silver-eyed, Where stars upon their courses glide, And night unfolds her mantle wide Above the depths of Xibalba. His face was pale, his light was fair, And silver flowed about his hair, As softly through the evening air He watched the earth and Xibalba.

The moon arose above the hill, And shone upon the waters still, And bathed the sleeping fields until The darkness seemed to lose its will Beyond the courts of Xibalba. No longer was the night alone, Nor every shadow death's to own, For gentle light upon it shone Above the halls of Xibalba.

And from the dark a thousand gleams Appeared like sparks upon the streams, As though the heavens dreamed the dreams Of those who slept beneath the beams Beyond the depths of Xibalba. Four hundred youths of ancient days, Once slain beneath the mountain's gaze, Arose to fill the starry ways Above the courts of Xibalba.

The stars were scattered far and wide, And shone upon the ocean tide, And watched the moon in silver glide, While all the heavens glorified The fallen might of Xibalba. Their lights were small, their numbers great, And none had power to close their gate, Or bind them to the mortal fate Once ruled by kings of Xibalba.

Then all the world was clothed in light, By golden day and silver night, And heaven wheeled beyond the sight Of those who once had feared the might Of all the kings of Xibalba. The birds arose upon the wing, The rivers found their voice to sing, And every green and growing thing Awoke beyond Xibalba.

The people watched the morning rise, And saw the glory of the skies, And wondered at the great surprise Of light that filled their waking eyes Above the depths of Xibalba. No word was spoken of the slain, No voice recalled the ancient pain, Yet all who sowed the fields of grain Were freed from dread Xibalba.

And when the sun began to fall, And twilight spread its purple pall, And evening birds began to call Across the mountains, dark and tall, Above the gates of Xibalba, The silver moon arose again, And watched the sleeping world of men, While stars returned to heaven then Beyond the reach of Xibalba.

And evermore the brothers played, Across the heavens, light and shade, While day and night their courses made, And neither ever seemed afraid Of ancient dark Xibalba. The golden ball of day was thrown, Across the heavens, bright and lone, And silver answered from her throne Above the courts of Xibalba.

And far beneath the roots of trees, Where never blows the summer breeze, The vanquished kings upon their knees Remembered still the old decrees That once had ruled Xibalba. No more their voices shook the stone, No more they claimed the world their own, But heard the waters passing on Through silent, dark Xibalba.

The fathers rested in the deep, Where ancient waters softly creep, And seeds within the darkness sleep, Till spring shall bid their green shoots leap Above the stones of Xibalba. For life must fall before it grows, And every summer meets its close, Yet from the earth the maize arose Beyond the death of Xibalba.

So when the fields are bare and brown, And all the summer leaves fall down, And winter wears her withered crown, The roots remember, deep below, The ancient halls of Xibalba. For what is buried is not gone, Nor every night without a dawn, And life shall rise and journey on Beyond the gates of Xibalba.

And still above the mountain height, The brothers keep their ancient flight, One clothed in gold, one silver-bright, And neither yields his watch to night That once belonged to Xibalba. The morning comes, the evening falls, And through the years their journey calls, While time itself remembers all The sons who conquered Xibalba.

Beneath the earth, beneath the stone, Where rivers carve the dark unknown, The kings of death have lost their throne, And all the seeds their hands had sown Lie silent now in Xibalba. But high above the fields of grain, Beyond the reach of death and pain, Two brothers rise, and rise again, Forever free of Xibalba.

End of Book 7
