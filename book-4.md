# The Twins of Xibalba

## Book IV — The Borrowed Head

The night lay heavy on the stone,  
And Xbalanque remained alone,  
Beside his brother, overthrown,  
Whose severed head the kings had shown  
Before the courts of Xibalba.  
No tears he shed, no cry he gave,  
Though death had opened wide the grave,  
For still he knew that thought might save  
A soul from dark Xibalba.

He called the creatures of the wood,  
Who came before him where he stood,  
And bade them offer what they could,  
That he might turn their gifts to good  
Against the kings of Xibalba.  
The forest stirred with foot and wing,  
And each came forth with what to bring,  
Yet none had brought the needful thing  
To conquer death in Xibalba.

A coati came, small and brown,  
Who rolled a squash before him down,  
As round and smooth as any crown  
That ever graced a king's renown  
Within the halls of Xibalba.  
And Xbalanque beheld its face,  
And lifted it with solemn grace,  
And set it in his brother's place  
Beneath the eyes of Xibalba.

Then from the heavens, far and high,  
The Heart of Sky drew softly nigh,  
And lent the fruit a living eye,  
And breathed where mortal lips were dry  
Below the courts of Xibalba.  
The features formed beneath his hand,  
The breath returned at his command,  
And Hunahpu arose to stand  
Among the dead of Xibalba.

But dawn approached the eastern height,  
And still the face was not quite right,  
When Heaven veiled the coming light,  
And soot was cast across the white  
And distant roofs of Xibalba.  
The morning faltered in its flight,  
The stars returned against the night,  
Till every feature stood aright  
Before the dawn of Xibalba.

The brothers spoke in whispers low,  
Of what the kings would have them know,  
Of where the ball of death might go,  
And how a trick might overthrow  
The mighty lords of Xibalba.  
A rabbit heard their secret plan,  
And through the garden lightly ran,  
And hid where scarlet berries span  
The outer walls of Xibalba.

The morning came, the trumpets cried,  
The gates were opened far and wide,  
And all the kings came forth in pride,  
To see the brother they defied  
Return to play in Xibalba.  
They hung his head before the crowd,  
And laughed in voices fierce and loud,  
And stood beneath the banners proud  
Above the courts of Xibalba.

Then Hunahpu, with fearless eye,  
Looked on the head suspended high,  
And heard the kings their triumph cry,  
Yet gave no grief and breathed no sigh  
Before the thrones of Xibalba.  
“Come, strike the ball, and let us play!  
The dead have little more to say;  
Let skill decide the prize today  
Upon the courts of Xibalba.”

The ball was cast against the wall,  
And Xbalanque returned the ball,  
And sent it flying past them all,  
Beyond the court's enclosing wall,  
Among the fields of Xibalba.  
It struck the earth and bounded wide,  
Beyond the lords in all their pride,  
Who thought to seize their prize outside  
And claim it there in Xibalba.

Then from the leaves a rabbit leapt,  
And through the garden swiftly swept;  
The lords forgot the watch they kept,  
And from the court in fury stepped  
And chased it through all Xibalba.  
They chased it down the winding way,  
Past stones that knew no light of day,  
And left the brothers free to stay  
Upon the courts of Xibalba.

Then Xbalanque, with nimble hand,  
Brought down the head above the stand,  
And wrought the healing they had planned  
Beyond the reach of death's command  
Before the courts of Xibalba.  
And Hunahpu beheld the light,  
And knew his brother stood aright,  
And laughed aloud at death's despite  
Within the courts of Xibalba.

The borrowed head rolled on the ground,  
And there it lay without a sound,  
While all the kings came back around,  
And angry voices gathered round  
To claim the courts of Xibalba,  
The brothers stood as once before,  
And challenged death to play once more,  
And struck the squash across the floor  
Before the kings of Xibalba.

The ball flew high, the ball flew low,  
And neither side would yield a throw,  
Until a sudden mighty blow  
Sent seeds like scattered drops of snow  
Across the courts of Xibalba.  
The yellow flesh was torn apart,  
And spilled its seeds before the start  
Of cries that struck each royal heart  
With shame in dark Xibalba.

The kings beheld the broken rind,  
And knew their eyes had been made blind,  
By cunning of a greater kind  
Than all the arts their courts designed  
To rule the souls of Xibalba.  
No word was spoken from the throne,  
No boast of victory was known,  
And every lord stood still as stone  
The wounded pride of Xibalba.

Yet One Death watched with burning hate,  
And Seven Death beside him sate,  
And both resolved to seal the fate  
Of those who mocked their ancient state  
Before the thrones of Xibalba.  
They whispered where the torches burned,  
And none who heard their counsel turned,  
For dreadful was the doom discerned  
Within the halls of Xibalba.

A furnace stood beneath the ground,  
Where walls of stone were built around,  
And fire consumed without a sound  
Whatever living flesh was bound  
To enter flames in Xibalba.  
There neither craft nor shield might save,  
Nor wit withstand the burning grave,  
And death at last would have the brave  
Who challenged him in Xibalba.

But Hunahpu had seen the flame,  
And Xbalanque had guessed the game,  
And both perceived the kings would claim  
By fire what none could win through shame  
Within the courts of Xibalba.  
They sought two seers, both old and wise,  
Who read the stars with clouded eyes,  
And knew what forms might yet arise  
Beyond the death of Xibalba.

To Xulu first the brothers spoke,  
And Pacam heard the words they woke,  
Of bodies burned and bones once broke,  
Of ashes scattered like the smoke  
Above the streams of Xibalba.  
“When all our mortal flesh is slain,  
And only shattered bones remain,  
Then grind them fine as falling rain,  
And cast them forth in Xibalba.”

“Let not the kings erect a stone,  
Nor bury us where none have grown;  
Let all our dust be downward thrown  
Where dark and restless waters moan  
Beneath the earth of Xibalba.  
For what the river takes away,  
It bears beyond the reach of clay,  
And death shall learn another day  
The secret kept from Xibalba.”

Then Xbalanque beheld his twin,  
And spoke of all that they might win,  
If both should dare the fire within,  
And let the final game begin  
Before the kings of Xibalba.  
No fear was in his brother's face,  
No longing for the upper place,  
For both had come to end the race  
Of tyrants ruling Xibalba.

The furnace roared beneath the floor,  
The kings assembled there once more,  
And bade the twins approach the door,  
As none had dared approach before  
The burning heart of Xibalba.  
And Hunahpu, with steady eye,  
Beheld the fire mounting high,  
Then heard his brother's soft reply:  
“We die to conquer Xibalba.”
