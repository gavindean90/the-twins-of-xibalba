Book 5 of VII

The Fire and the Resurrection

The furnace roared with tongues of red, Its light upon the pillars spread, And all the kings of darkness led The brothers where the flames were fed Beneath the halls of Xibalba. No mortal foot had crossed that floor, And walked among the living more, For death had sealed the burning door Against the sons of Xibalba.

The lords arose with cries of glee, And called the twins their sport to see, For none who lived could hope to flee The fire that burned eternally Within the depths of Xibalba. But Hunahpu beheld the flame, And Xbalanque, who knew the same, Stepped forth as though to play a game Before the kings of Xibalba.

They clasped their hands before the throne, And heard the furnace roar and moan, While One Death spoke in hollow tone Of all the souls he called his own Throughout the lands of Xibalba. Then laughed the twins, and forward sprang, And through the hall their laughter rang, As every watching trumpet sang The triumph of Xibalba.

Into the flames the brothers flew, And scarlet sparks around them grew, Till neither face the watchers knew, And all their mortal forms withdrew Within the fires of Xibalba. The kings cried out with savage cheer, For all their trials ended here, And nevermore would men draw near To mock the thrones of Xibalba.

The flames grew pale, the embers died, And ash lay scattered far and wide; The kings came forth in boastful pride, And saw no brothers side by side Before the thrones of Xibalba. No living heart beneath the stone, No laughing voice, no answering tone, And death believed the world his own Beneath the earth of Xibalba.

But Xulu heard the kings proclaim, And Pacam knew the hidden game, For they had heard before the flame The brothers speak of death and shame Among the lords of Xibalba. They gathered bones from where they lay, And bore them silently away, Before the coming of the day Could stir the courts of Xibalba.

They ground the bones to powder white, And worked beneath the veiled starlight, Until the dust was fine and light, And ready for the river's flight Beyond the walls of Xibalba. No grave was dug, no stone was laid, No offering to death was made, But all the ashes were conveyed Into the streams of Xibalba.

The river took the ashen grain, And bore it through its dark domain, Beyond the reach of mortal pain, Where neither sun nor summer rain Could touch the roots of Xibalba. And all the while the waters rolled, Through caverns deep and caverns cold, And kept a secret never told Among the kings of Xibalba.

Five days beneath the waters passed, Five days the current held them fast, Until the hidden spell was cast, And life awoke from death at last Beneath the streams of Xibalba. Two shapes arose from depths unseen, Where never living men had been, And swam through waters dark and green Below the courts of Xibalba.

Not men they were, nor spirits pale, But creatures bearing fin and tail, Who swam against the rushing gale Of waters pouring through the vale Beneath the caves of Xibalba. They turned where ancient currents sweep, And rose from out the silent deep, Where death believed their bones would sleep Forevermore in Xibalba.

Another dawn, another day, The waters bore their forms away, And on the dark and stony clay Two ragged strangers came to stay Among the folk of Xibalba. Their garments hung in tattered thread, With neither crown upon the head, And none could name the risen dead Who walked the roads of Xibalba.

They wandered where the people came, And neither spoke his father's name, Nor sought the honor or the fame That once had stirred the ancient game Before the kings of Xibalba. They danced upon the market floor, And sang of wonders known before, Till crowds assembled more and more Throughout the towns of Xibalba.

They danced beneath the cavern height, And made the stones appear as light, And turned their bodies left and right, Till all who watched forgot the night That brooded over Xibalba. They leaped as though the air were ground, And spun where neither rope was bound, And filled the courts with such a sound As none had heard in Xibalba.

Then Hunahpu, with smiling face, Brought forth a dog before the place, And struck it down with sudden grace, While silence fell upon the space Among the crowds of Xibalba. The creature lay upon the floor, And drew its living breath no more, Till Xbalanque restored once more Its life before Xibalba.

The dog arose and shook its head, And bounded where its blood had spread, As though it never had been dead, And every watching stranger said A wonder walked in Xibalba. The people cried with wild delight, And pressed to see another sight, While whispers hurried through the night To all the lords of Xibalba.

They built a house of timber dry, Whose roof was raised against the sky, And bade the watchers standing by Observe the flames that leaped on high Above the stones of Xibalba. The rafters fell, the walls were flame, And all the house a ruin became, Till at a word it stood the same Before the eyes of Xibalba.

The people marveled at the sight, And asked what power gave them might To call the dead from endless night, And build from ashes burning bright Within the lands of Xibalba. But neither brother spoke a word, Of ancient vows the mountains heard, Or of the fire that once had stirred The mighty kings of Xibalba.

Then Xbalanque drew forth a blade, And Hunahpu before him stayed, And neither trembled nor delayed To show what wonders might be made Before the eyes of Xibalba. The blade descended swift and deep, And laid his brother down to sleep, While all the watching women weep Within the courts of Xibalba.

His arms were parted from his side, His head was severed, cast aside, And all his limbs lay scattered wide, While Xbalanque in silence eyed The watching crowds of Xibalba. Then from the earth he raised his hand, And spoke a word of stern command, And bade his brother rise and stand Before the folk of Xibalba.

The scattered limbs together flew, The severed head its likeness knew, And breath within the body grew, Till Hunahpu stood living, new, Before the crowds of Xibalba. He bowed before the gathered throng, And laughed as though at nothing wrong, And all the people raised a song That thundered through Xibalba.

No mortal eye had seen the like, No mortal hand could deal the strike, And summon life where death held right To rule the everlasting night Among the souls of Xibalba. From mouth to mouth the story flew, And all the tales of wonder grew, Till even kings desired to view The strangers of Xibalba.

Within their halls the lords reclined, With crowns upon their brows entwined, And heard the wonders men had signed As works beyond the mortal mind Throughout the lands of Xibalba. Then One Death spoke with eager breath, And Seven Death forgot his wrath, For both desired to learn the path That conquered death in Xibalba.

“Bring forth these men of wondrous skill, Who raise the dead and bend the will Of fire and flesh, and hold them still, And make the shattered body fill With living breath in Xibalba. Let them perform before our throne, And show what arts are theirs alone, For such a power should be known Unto the kings of Xibalba.”

The summons passed from hall to hall, And reached the brothers, bent and small, Who heard the royal herald call, And knew the hour approached for all The ancient kings of Xibalba. They wrapped themselves in garments torn, And wore the faces they had worn When from the river they were born To walk once more in Xibalba.

They came before the palace gate, Where death had sealed their father's fate, And where the brothers long had sate Beneath the terrors dark and great That guarded ancient Xibalba. No golden mantle clothed them now, No feathered crown adorned the brow, And none who saw their humble bow Knew whom they faced in Xibalba.

The kings beheld their ragged guise, And mocked the strangers' downcast eyes, And bade them stand before the wise And mighty lords, whose dark decrees Commanded all of Xibalba. Then Xbalanque began to smile, And Hunahpu stood still awhile, For death had walked another mile Into the snare of Xibalba.

The torches burned against the stone, The kings assembled round the throne, And bade the strangers make it known What wondrous arts were theirs alone Before the court of Xibalba. The brothers bowed before them all, And waited for the royal call, While shadows lengthened on the wall Within the halls of Xibalba.

Then One Death raised his sceptered hand, And gave the brothers his command: “Perform the wonders of your land, And let the dead before us stand Within the courts of Xibalba. For we have heard the tales men tell, Of those who break the mortal spell, And summon souls from where they dwell Beyond the gates of Xibalba.”

And Hunahpu looked toward his twin, Who knew the game they now must win, And both concealed the joy within, For death himself had called them in Before the thrones of Xibalba. The ancient kings leaned forward then, And called for life from death again, Unknowing that the sons of men Had come to conquer Xibalba.

End of Book 5
