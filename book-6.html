Book 6 of VII

The Fall of the Lords of Death

Within the hall of carven stone, The lords sat high on seats of bone, And summoned to their awful throne Two wanderers, poor and all unknown, Before the kings of Xibalba. They stood beneath the torches bright, Their faces hidden from the light, And none foresaw the coming night That waited there in Xibalba.

The brothers danced upon the clay, And sang the ancient hours away, Till One Death bade their music stay, And brought a hound before them, gray, Within the courts of Xibalba. They struck it down upon the floor, Where life had filled its limbs before, Then bade it rise and live once more Before the thrones of Xibalba.

The creature leapt upon its feet, And bounded through the crowded street, As though no death had been to meet, Nor mortal wound its heart to beat Within the halls of Xibalba. The kings arose with wild delight, And cried to see another sight, That life might triumph over night Before the thrones of Xibalba.

Then Xbalanque approached the wall, And bade the servants leave the hall, And set the rafters, great and tall, Ablaze before the eyes of all The mighty kings of Xibalba. The flames arose in tongues of red, And through the vaulted ceiling spread, Till roof and timber fell like lead Upon the floors of Xibalba.

The pillars cracked, the chambers glowed, And fiery rivers downward flowed, Till nothing of the palace showed But ashes where its masters strode Among the halls of Xibalba. Then Hunahpu upraised his hand, And spoke a word of stern command, And all the palace rose to stand Before the kings of Xibalba.

The walls returned, the rafters grew, The golden lamps their places knew, And every painted stone withdrew Its scars, and shone as though brand-new Within the courts of Xibalba. The kings cried out with one accord, And praised the wonders they adored, Till One Death rose, their eldest lord, Upon the throne of Xibalba.

“Have ye no greater art to show? No deeper mystery to know? Can ye command the blood to flow, And bid the fallen rise below The ancient roofs of Xibalba? For we have seen the burning flame, And beasts restored from whence they came; But men are worthier of fame Before the kings of Xibalba.”

Then Xbalanque drew forth a blade, And Hunahpu before him stayed, And neither trembled nor delayed To show the wonder death had made Within the courts of Xibalba. The blade descended, keen and bright, And struck his brother from the light, Till all the kings beheld the sight With wonder in Xibalba.

He severed limb from living limb, And all the torches flickered dim, And every watching face grew grim As death descended over him Before the thrones of Xibalba. His heart was taken from his breast, His head beside his body pressed, And Hunahpu lay still at rest Upon the stones of Xibalba.

Then Xbalanque began to sing, And danced within the charmed ring, And called upon the hidden spring From which the breath of life might bring Its blessing down to Xibalba. The scattered members stirred anew, The severed head its features drew, And through the veins the warm blood flew Before the kings of Xibalba.

Then Hunahpu arose once more, And stood upon the palace floor, As living as he stood before, And laughed before the open door Of death within Xibalba. The brothers joined their hands in dance, And all the kings were in a trance, As life itself appeared to glance With mockery at Xibalba.

The lords forgot their ancient pride, Forgot the hosts their terrors tried, And crowded to the brothers' side, Their eager longing undisguised Before the thrones of Xibalba. For death had ruled the world below, Yet never learned the way to go Beyond the grave, and rise to show New life in dark Xibalba.

Then One Death rose and gave a cry, And raised his golden scepter high, “Let me beneath your weapons lie, And from the dead return, as I Command the souls of Xibalba! For if a mortal may be slain, And rise to greet the world again, Then surely kings may break the chain That binds the dead of Xibalba!”

And Seven Death beside him rose, And cast aside his jeweled clothes, And cried, “Let all the kingdom know That we shall die and rise to show The sovereign might of Xibalba! Come, strike us down, and raise us then, That all may see the kings again, And death be master over men Throughout the lands of Xibalba!”

The brothers bowed before the throne, And spoke in voices soft and low, Of arts that death had never known, And seeds that must be deeply sown Before they bloom in Xibalba. Then Hunahpu drew forth the blade, And Xbalanque the circle made, And none among the kings was afraid Within the halls of Xibalba.

Then One Death laid his scepter down, And cast aside his golden crown, And smiled upon the gathered town, As though immortal his renown Throughout the lands of Xibalba. He stretched himself upon the stone, And bade them make his wonder known, And all the lords approached the throne To watch the king of Xibalba.

The blade descended swift and deep, And One Death sank to endless sleep; No cry arose, no soul did weep, For all believed that he would leap Again to rule in Xibalba. His blood ran dark upon the stone, His crown lay fallen from the throne, And silence filled the courts alone Beneath the halls of Xibalba.

The brothers stood beside the dead, And neither spoke, nor bowed his head, Nor called the spirit that had fled To rise and walk where once it tread Before the kings of Xibalba. The moments passed, the torches burned, The waiting lords their faces turned, And One Death neither moved nor stirred Upon the stones of Xibalba.

Then Seven Death arose in fear, And cried, “What dreadful thing is here? Why does my brother not appear? Why does his voice no longer cheer The gathered lords of Xibalba?” But Hunahpu approached him slow, And spoke of things he did not know, And bade the trembling monarch go Before the thrones of Xibalba.

The king drew back, his courage fled, And stared upon the royal dead, And all the words he might have said Were silenced by the rising dread That filled the halls of Xibalba. He begged the brothers spare his breath, And cried that he had feared no death, Till now he saw the shadow's breadth Before his throne in Xibalba.

But Xbalanque approached the throne, And took the lord who stood alone, And laid him where the blood had flown From One Death, cold upon the stone, Before the kings of Xibalba. The blade arose, the blade was cast, And Seven Death breathed forth his last, And all his vaunted power passed Into the dust of Xibalba.

No song arose, no dance was made, No hand restored the fallen blade, And neither brother seemed afraid Of what their hands had now unmade Before the thrones of Xibalba. The two great kings lay side by side, Their golden robes with blood were dyed, And all the lesser lords who cried Fled screaming through Xibalba.

Through vaulted halls the terror spread, And every servant bowed his head, And all who once commanded dread Now fled before the nameless dead Who conquered kings in Xibalba. They ran beyond the palace wall, They stumbled where the shadows fall, And heard the brothers' voices call Across the lands of Xibalba.

They crowded in the cavern deep, Where ancient rivers downward sweep, And piled like flocks of frightened sheep Upon the rocks, where none could creep Beyond the bounds of Xibalba. Then from the cracks the ants arose, And swarmed about their trembling toes, And barred the path by which they chose To flee the wrath of Xibalba.

The lords fell weeping to the ground, And cried for mercy all around, For never had the mighty found Their pride so utterly unbound Within the courts of Xibalba. They tore their robes and bowed their heads, And cursed the thrones where once they led, And wished themselves among the dead Who slept beneath Xibalba.

Then Hunahpu stepped forth in light, And Xbalanque stood at his right, And both were clothed in splendor bright, No longer beggars of the night Who wandered through Xibalba. The rags fell from their shoulders bare, And jeweled feathers crowned their hair, And all the kings beheld them there As masters of Xibalba.

“Behold us now, ye lords of stone, Who sit upon the stolen throne, And think that death is yours alone, And all beneath the earth your own By ancient right in Xibalba! We are the sons of him ye slew, Whose blood upon your altars flew, And now the reckoning comes due Before the kings of Xibalba.”

Then Hunahpu with steady eye Looked on the kings who dared not rise, And spoke beneath the vaulted sky Of ancient blood and treachery Within the courts of Xibalba. “One Hunahpu, our father, came, And Seven Hunahpu the same, And both were caught within your game And murdered here in Xibalba.”

“Ye summoned them with honeyed tongue, And praised the songs their courage sung, Yet all your snares were closely strung To slay the noble and the young Who entered dark Xibalba. Their heads were taken from the slain, Their bodies cast beneath the plain, And none returned to earth again From out the gates of Xibalba.”

The brothers' voices filled the hall, And echoed from the cavern wall, And every lord who heard the call Fell lower still before them all Upon the stones of Xibalba. No scepter now could make them brave, No guard could rescue or could save, For all their power found its grave Beneath the earth of Xibalba.

Then Xbalanque pronounced their doom, And through the vaulted halls of gloom His words rang out from room to room, And seemed to shake the ancient tomb That held the dead of Xibalba. “Your days of glory now are done, Your wars against the living won No honor underneath the sun For any lord of Xibalba.”

“No more shall noble blood be shed, To honor kings among the dead, Nor shall the living bow the head And bring the innocent instead To feed the thrones of Xibalba. No longer shall your altars shine With offerings of the sacred vine, Nor shall the sons of men resign Their lives to dark Xibalba.”

“The resin of the trees shall burn, And lesser gifts shall be your turn, But nevermore shall mankind learn To fear the glories ye would earn Within the courts of Xibalba. The meanest creatures shall be yours, The broken things the world abhors, And none shall praise your cruel wars Upon the plains of Xibalba.”

“Your painted stones shall fade away, Your golden courts shall turn to clay, Your names shall wither and decay, And darkness shall consume the day That once was yours in Xibalba. No temples raised beneath the sun, No songs of victories ye have won, For all your ancient reign is done Within the halls of Xibalba.”

The kings bent low beneath the word, And not a voice among them stirred, For all their hearts had plainly heard The doom pronounced, the judgment heard Against the might of Xibalba. They bowed before the brothers' feet, And knew their overthrow complete, And tasted shame, more sharp than sweet, Upon the stones of Xibalba.

Then through the halls a silence fell, And none remained their grief to tell, Save distant waters in the dell, And echoes of the funeral bell That sounded deep in Xibalba. The thrones were empty, cold and bare, And dust lay on the royal chair, And none of all the lords would dare To reign again in Xibalba.

Yet Hunahpu beheld the ground, Where once the rubber ball had bound The brothers to the hollow sound Of feet that beat the earth around The ancient courts of Xibalba. He thought upon his father's face, And of the long-forgotten place, Where death had left no living trace Of those it slew in Xibalba.

Then Xbalanque approached his side, And looked beyond the cavern wide, Where ancient waters downward glide, And shadows of the dead abide Beneath the roots of Xibalba. “Our work is not yet done,” he said, “For though the kings of death have fled, Our father sleeps among the dead Beneath the courts of Xibalba.”

They turned away from golden throne, And left the silent halls of stone, And sought the place where bones were strewn, Where grief had lain so long alone Within the depths of Xibalba. The path was dark, the road was deep, And through the stones the waters creep, Where ancient fathers lay asleep Beyond the thrones of Xibalba.

And far above, the fields of grain Bent softly in the summer rain, While sunlight touched the earth again, And none who walked the living plain Could see the fall of Xibalba. Yet something stirred beneath the ground, A silence deeper than the sound Of all the kings whose power was bound Forevermore in Xibalba.

For death had learned what life could do, And ancient bonds were broken through, And where the roots of maize once grew, A promise waited to renew The world beyond Xibalba. The brothers walked where shadows cease, And sought their father's long release, While all the kingdom slept in peace Beneath the stones of Xibalba.

End of Book 6
