# The Twins of Xibalba

## Book III — The Houses of Death

The dawn crept pale across the floor,  
The kings assembled there once more,  
And bade the twins approach the door,  
Where sire and uncle played before  
Within the courts of Xibalba.  
The rubber ball rose black and high,  
And beat against the hollow sky,  
While death looked on with watchful eye  
Above the courts of Xibalba.

They drove the ball from side to side,  
And death looked on in swelling pride,  
Until its painted seams flew wide,  
And from within a knife did glide  
Across the court of Xibalba.  
Then Hunahpu declared the game  
Was fit for cowards lost to shame;  
The kings, afraid to bear the blame,  
Called peace in dark Xibalba.

They played again till shadows fell,  
And heard the hollow evening bell,  
When One Death spoke beneath his spell:  
“And should ye lose, ye know full well  
The price we ask in Xibalba.  
Four bowls of flowers, fresh and fair,  
From gardens none may enter there,  
Though watchful owls infest the air  
Above the walls of Xibalba.”

The twins were led where blades were bright,  
Where knives flew wheeling through the night,  
And every edge was keen to bite,  
And none had lived to greet the light  
Who slept within Xibalba.  
Xbalanque bade their fury stay,  
And promised flesh another day,  
And all their edges turned away  
Within the halls of Xibalba.

He called the ants from crack and seam,  
Who moved like one unbroken stream,  
And crossed the gardens in a dream,  
Where flowers shone with moonlit gleam  
Behind the walls of Xibalba.  
They bore the blossoms, red and white,  
Past owls who guarded through the night,  
And left the gardens bare by light  
Before the kings of Xibalba.

At dawn they brought the blossoms fair,  
And laid their bowls with solemn care;  
The kings looked on in blank despair,  
For all their guarded beds were bare  
Beyond the walls of Xibalba.  
The owls hung heads beneath their gaze,  
The lords were struck with sore amaze,  
And all their cunning seemed a haze  
Within the courts of Xibalba.

They drove them to a house of snow,  
Where hail fell thick and winds did blow,  
Where every breath hung pale and slow,  
And buried roots forgot to grow  
Beneath the earth of Xibalba.  
But embers warmed the bitter air,  
And threw their light on faces fair,  
And dawn beheld the brothers there  
Alive in cold Xibalba.

A hundred jaguars watched with eyes  
Like amber stars in moonless skies;  
Their hunger rose in savage cries,  
And death awaited for his prize  
Inside the dens of Xibalba.  
The brothers cast them bones to gnaw,  
And watched each cruel and mighty jaw  
Grow still beneath a gentler law  
Within the caves of Xibalba.

Then came the house of living flame,  
Where walls and floor were all the same,  
Where fire had neither shape nor name,  
And every spark a soul might claim  
To feed the fires of Xibalba.  
The flames rose red against the wall,  
But neither twin was seen to fall,  
At dawn they answered to the call  
Before the kings of Xibalba.

The kings grew pale upon the throne,  
Their vaunted power overthrown,  
For every terror they had sown  
Returned to mock their might alone  
Within the halls of Xibalba.  
But One Death rose in bitter spite,  
And called the creatures of the night,  
Whose wings could sever flesh outright  
Within the depths of Xibalba.

They entered where no candles shone,  
Where shrieking wings had made their own  
A kingdom carved from living stone,  
And neither brother stood alone  
Among the bats of Xibalba.  
Within their blowguns safe they lay,  
And hid from wings that sought their prey,  
And watched the hours waste away  
Within the dark of Xibalba.

The hours passed without a sound,  
The wings grew still, the shadows drowned  
The vaulted roof, the stony ground,  
And silence settled all around  
The sleeping bats of Xibalba.  
Then Hunahpu, with cautious eye,  
Looked forth to seek the waking sky,  
And thought the dawn was drawing nigh  
Above the roofs of Xibalba.

But Camazotz awoke in flight,  
His wings were blacker than the night,  
His sharpened snout, a blade of fright,  
Descended swift beyond their sight  
Within the depths of Xibalba.  
And ere the brother turned his head,  
The stone beneath was stained with red,  
And Hunahpu lay with the dead  
Before the dawn in Xibalba.

The lords looked down in dark delight,  
And bade the torchless court be bright,  
For there the head was raised in sight,  
A trophy of the dreadful night  
Before the thrones of Xibalba.  
Yet Xbalanque, in silence deep,  
Refused the bitter tears to weep;  
For promises the brothers keep  
Beyond the death of Xibalba.
