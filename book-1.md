# The Twins of Xibalba

## Book I — The Summons

Beneath the mountains, cold and deep,  
Where ancient kings their councils keep,  
Where roots through halls of darkness creep,  
And rivers through the silence sweep,  
There lie the courts of Xibalba.  
No morning breaks upon the stone,  
No living wind has ever blown,  
And death upon his shadowed throne  
Commands the halls of Xibalba.

Above, where fields of maize grew green,  
A sacred ballcourt lay between  
The hills, its sloping walls swept clean,  
Where sire and uncle once had been  
Before their deaths in Xibalba.  
A rubber ball, both black and round,  
They struck from wall to beaten ground;  
Its thunder rolled beneath the mound  
And roused the kings of Xibalba.

That sound had reached them once before,  
When those two brothers kept the score;  
The lords had called them through the door,  
And spilled their blood upon the floor  
Within the courts of Xibalba.  
The kings arose in wrath and dread,  
And called their servants from the dead;  
To seek the twins, the servants sped  
Across the earth o’er Xibalba.

Through tangled root and forest shade,  
Through fields where ancient gods had played,  
The messengers their journey made,  
And found the brothers undismayed  
Before the gates of Xibalba.  
They spoke the summons of the throne,  
Of trials hard and terrors known,  
And warned that none returned alone  
Who entered dark Xibalba.

Then Hunahpu, with steady eye,  
Looked toward the mountains standing high,  
Where clouds concealed the distant sky,  
And heard his brother's soft reply:  
“We go to meet Xibalba.”  
No sword they took, no shield they wore,  
But wisdom gathered long before,  
And blowguns hidden by the door  
Would go with them to Xibalba.

Before they took the downward way,  
Two canes they planted in the clay,  
And told Xmucane where they lay:  
“By these you'll know our fate each day  
While we are gone to Xibalba.  
If dry, then death has closed its door;  
If green, we live and strive once more.”  
They took the blowguns that they bore  
And journeyed toward Xibalba.
