# The Twins of Xibalba

## Book I — The Summons

Beneath the mountains, cold and deep,  
Where ancient kings their councils keep,  
Where roots through halls of darkness creep,  
And rivers through the silence sweep,  
There lie the courts of Xibalba.  
No morning breaks upon the stone,  
No living wind has ever blown,  
And death upon his shadowed throne  
Commands the halls of Xibalba.

Above, where fields of maize were green,  
And golden sunlight fell between  
The leaves that clothed the valley's sheen,  
Two brothers walked, as yet unseen  
By all the kings of Xibalba.  
Their father's blood was in their veins,  
Their father's grief among their pains,  
And far below, in dark domains,  
There stirred the lords of Xibalba.

Upon the court the ball was cast,  
And through the hills its thunder passed,  
And down through caverns deep and vast,  
Till death awoke and held it fast  
Within the courts of Xibalba.  
The kings arose in wrath and dread,  
And called their servants from the dead,  
And bade them seek the twins who tread  
The road above Xibalba.

Through tangled root and forest shade,  
Through fields where ancient gods had played,  
The messengers their journey made,  
And found the brothers undismayed  
Before the gates of Xibalba.  
They spoke the summons of the throne,  
Of trials cruel and terrors known,  
And warned that none returned alone  
Who entered dark Xibalba.

Then Hunahpu, with steady eye,  
Looked toward the mountains standing high,  
Where clouds concealed the distant sky,  
And heard his brother's soft reply:  
“We go to meet Xibalba.”  
No sword they took, no shield they bore,  
But wisdom gathered long before,  
And turned their footsteps toward the door  
That opened on Xibalba.
