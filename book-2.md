# The Twins of Xibalba

## Book II — The Descent

The brothers left the fields of grain,  
And crossed the hills, and crossed the plain,  
Where sunlight fell like golden rain,  
And none who walked that road again  
Returned from dark Xibalba.  
Behind them lay the world of men,  
The flowering field, the wooded glen,  
Before them yawned the shadowed den  
That opened on Xibalba.

They came where blackened rivers rolled,  
Where waters ran through caverns cold,  
And whispered tales of warriors bold,  
Whose bones lay scattered, white and old,  
Along the road to Xibalba.  
The first ran thick with human blood,  
The second bore a deadly flood,  
Yet neither stained the brothers' blood  
Upon the way to Xibalba.

They crossed where ancient waters flowed,  
And found the dark descending road,  
Where neither star nor moonlight showed,  
And every step beneath them glowed  
With fires of deep Xibalba.  
Four roads before the brothers lay,  
One black, one white, one red, one gray,  
And each would lead the feet astray  
That journeyed toward Xibalba.

The black road spoke with honeyed tongue,  
Of songs the ancient dead had sung,  
Of crowns upon the mighty hung,  
And glories lost when earth was young  
Before the kings of Xibalba.  
But from a hair a gnat took flight,  
And vanished through the gathering night,  
To find the lords who sat in might  
Upon the thrones of Xibalba.

It flew among the silent dead,  
And bit the hands where kings were spread,  
And heard the names their fellows said,  
As each in sudden anger cried  
Within the halls of Xibalba.  
Thus every lord was named and known,  
And every false and wooden throne,  
Before the brothers stood alone  
Before the kings of Xibalba.

There sat the lords in solemn state,  
Whose laughter sealed the wanderer's fate,  
Who watched beside the iron gate,  
And bade the brothers come and wait  
Before the thrones of Xibalba.  
They offered seats of burning stone,  
And smiled to hear the victims groan,  
But found the brothers would not own  
The cunning of Xibalba.

Then One Death rose with hollow eyes,  
And spoke beneath the vaulted skies,  
“Though men be clever, none are wise  
Who come beneath the earth to rise  
Against the might of Xibalba.  
The night shall try what day hath made,  
The dark shall weigh the undismayed,  
And none shall leave the halls of shade  
Who challenge us in Xibalba.”

They gave the twins a torch of pine,  
And one cigar of leaf divine,  
And bade them keep the embers shine  
Till morning sent its feeble sign  
Through all the halls of Xibalba.  
Then led them where no star was bright,  
And sealed the doors against the light,  
And left them to the endless night  
Within the courts of Xibalba.

But Hunahpu beheld the flame,  
And knew the purpose of the game,  
And Xbalanque, who knew the same,  
Prepared to put their hosts to shame  
Before the dawn in Xibalba.  
They set a red macaw to glow,  
And fixed bright fireflies in a row,  
That all might think the flames did show  
Through all the night in Xibalba.

And when the morning shadows fled,  
The kings arose from halls of dread,  
And saw the torch, whose wood was red,  
And saw the leaf, whose fire seemed fed,  
Still burning bright in Xibalba.  
No coal was spent, no ash was cast,  
No flame had withered in the blast,  
And thus the brothers safely passed  
The first dark night in Xibalba.

Yet far beneath the roots of trees,  
Beyond the reach of summer breeze,  
Where death commanded all decrees,  
The kings conspired, ill at ease,  
Within the courts of Xibalba.  
For still the houses waited deep,  
Where jaguars watched and serpents creep,  
And bats hung dreaming in their sleep  
Above the halls of Xibalba.
