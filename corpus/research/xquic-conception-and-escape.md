# Xquic’s Conception and Escape

## Narrative sequence — source evidence

Xquic hears about the tree from her father Cuchumaquic, an underworld lord. She goes alone, examines the fruit and answers the head when it questions her desire. At its request she extends her right hand. The head directs saliva into it; when she inspects her palm, the saliva is no longer visible. The head explains continuity through sons and daughters and directs her toward the earth. The account associates these events with the will of Huracán and the other named powers. She returns home; conception is attributed to the saliva. After six months, her father notices the pregnancy. M 15v–16r, sequences 40–41; S pp. 38–39; B II.iii, pp. 90–95; `pv-xquic-conception`.

Her father consults the ruling pair. She denies knowledge of a man, but an execution order follows. Four owl messengers are to bring back her heart in a vessel and carry a cutting instrument. She argues against her killing, explains the head encounter and confronts the practical problem of what the messengers can return. She proposes the substitute: red tree sap collected and shaped into a heart-like mass. She also promises the messengers a future above. They present the substitute; the rulers burn it and are absorbed by its fragrance. The narrative explicitly credits the maiden with defeating/deceiving the lords. M 16r–17r, sequences 41–43; S pp. 39–42; B pp. 94–101; G February II.iii §§15–22 and March §§23–31; `pv-xquic-escape`.

## Primary evidence and controlled readings

Three kinds of evidence must remain distinct. First, the images locate the complete episode in both columns. Second, the short heading and conception clause have newly prepared [diplomatic working records](../metadata/transcriptions.json). Third, printed parallels give fuller searchable text while retaining their editorial mediation. The conception excerpt on 16r includes `rumal ri xachub` in the working reading; its gloss cautiously attributes the offspring’s coming into being to saliva alone. It is not normalized to a modern spelling, nor independently verified by a K’iche’ reader.

The head’s speech on 16r develops its image through the survival of a lord’s, wise person’s or speaker’s identity in descendants, including daughters. S p. 39 preserves the daughter/son extension. Saliva is therefore not just a mechanical plot device: the account itself gives an explanation in terms of lineage and continuity. Equating it with semen is an interpretive analogy; that equation is not supplied as a quoted manuscript word here.

## Historical comparisons

S p. 38 calls the saliva a sign. B p. 93 moves directly to posterity in the corresponding sentence; G February §9 follows that wording. P’s provisional OCR, PDF spread 24 left, retains the sign language. This difference belongs in the comparison even though the versions agree on conception. B’s French rendering of the hand omits the explicit right-side specification found in the manuscript Spanish; its facing K’iche’ preserves a phrase that needs linguistic analysis.

B p. 99 presents the substitute-heart transaction as a blood contract, and the notes turn the messengers’ promised future into a political conspiracy and property transfer. G carries related contract language. The manuscript Spanish on 16v–17r supports bargaining, red sap and deception, but does not by itself demonstrate Brasseur’s detailed historical-social reconstruction. Sp’s “bribing” compresses this negotiation and imposes a moral characterization. Describe what she promises before deciding how to label it.

The sap tree and skull tree should not silently become one botanical species. “Dragon’s blood,” “blood-wart,” red-tree terminology and specific scientific identifications belong to different editorial layers. This work establishes the material substitution, not a botanical identification.

## Modern philological checkpoint

Christenson, electronic edition pp. 114–115, note 276, contrasts his reading based on *chak* (work) with Sam Colop’s *ch’aq’* (flesh) for the skull’s condition. He considers both contextually possible. Colop’s 1999 p. 68 note 165 is cited here **through Christenson**, not claimed as directly consulted. This is a concrete reason to retain uncertainty in the head’s speech rather than modernize the old spelling automatically. See [references](references.md).

## Interpretation and implications for the poem

Xquic is an agent at each transition: she seeks knowledge, answers, disputes her condemnation, redirects the messengers and designs an effective substitute. Her escape anticipates the younger twins’ use of appearances against the rulers. That structural observation does not make her a disguised twin or prove a complete cosmological identity.

An expanded narrative needs room for the dialogue and negotiation, not merely a miraculous pregnancy followed by flight. Her father is Cuchumaquic, not One Death. Her denial of sexual wrongdoing should not be retold as a narrator’s judgment that she deceived her father about ordinary intercourse. No such event has been established.

## Outstanding research

Obtain specialist review of the saliva/descendants couplets, *chak/ch’aq’*, the right-hand phrase and the wording of the messengers’ promised future. Collate the attribution to both older brothers at the end of the skull speech. Establish the precise departure/guiding verbs: B’s party-of-supporters wording differs from the simpler Spanish outcome. Do not fill these gaps with a modern translation presented as manuscript transcription.
