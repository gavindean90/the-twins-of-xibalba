# The Head in the Tree

## Narrative sequence — source evidence

Following the first generation’s death, One Hunahpu’s body is buried with his brother. His severed head is placed among the branches of a roadside tree at the lords’ command. The previously unfruitful tree immediately bears rounded fruit. The head becomes indistinguishable among it. The rulers are astonished and impose a prohibition on approaching the tree or taking its fruit. News of the marvel reaches a maiden, introducing Xquic’s own episode. M 15r–15v, sequences 39–40; S pp. 37–38; B II.ii, pp. 88–91; `pv-head-tree`.

This order matters: the narrative moves from the lords’ treatment of the defeated body to an outcome they did not anticipate, then from that marvel to Xquic’s decision to investigate. Her arrival is causally connected to the transformed tree. It is not presented as a chance meeting with an otherwise unremarkable skull.

## Primary-source evidence

The K’iche’ passage on 15r repeatedly names One Hunahpu and the head; the parallel Spanish describes its separation, the body’s burial with the brother, placement in the tree and sudden fruit. The latter column continues on 15v with the prohibition and the hidden head. These are verified image locations, not a full diplomatic transcription. B’s printed K’iche’ on p. 88 offers a recoverable parallel at `brasseur-txt` lines 20438–20458; the complete mapped context extends through line 20536. This is Brasseur’s edited witness, explicitly not the manuscript text.

The short new diplomatic heading on 15v introduces the maiden as daughter of a lord named Cuchumaquic. It provides a limited checkable K’iche’ bridge into the next dossier. Its spelling and lineation are retained separately from any modern form of the names.

## Historical comparisons

S pp. 37–38 calls the fruit jícaras and includes the botanical parenthesis “Crescentia.” B pp. 89–91 calls the tree a calabash tree and discusses *tzima*. These belong to the historical witnesses; a modern species determination is not independently established by this research. The skull-bearing tree should not be merged with the squash used much later to replace the younger Hunahpu’s head. Similar rounded forms create a literary relation, but the episodes, objects and agents differ.

Sp, lines 357–365, compresses the fruiting and prohibition into a few sentences. G’s February installment, II.ii, retains the fuller transformation and transition to Xquic. Neither makes the head’s appearance in the tree independent corroboration of the manuscript: the manuscript supplies the primary scene, and the translation relationships remain important.

B’s surrounding notes interpret the houses as mysteries of initiation. His p. 91 note identifies Xquic with a Mexican divine figure. Such comparative identifications should not be inserted into a primary-source narrative summary. The local sequence establishes a young woman from Xibalba encountering the head; it does not require those identifications.

## Philological questions and interpretation

The proper name of the burial/tree locality recurs at the end of the twins’ adventure. Older versions render it as the Cendrier, cenicero or ash-heap. This is a translation question, not permission to claim an excavated archaeological ash dump. Preserve the K’iche’ place-name when discussing uncertain physical settings.

One head is physically singled out in the execution scene. Later, the speech’s attribution names One Hunahpu and Seven Hunahpu together (M 16r; B p. 94; S p. 39). That juxtaposition is real and deserves notice. It does not warrant drawing a second severed head into the scene or asserting a biological explanation for dual paternity. A collective ancestral voice is a possible interpretation, not a settled grammatical finding.

The source supports persistence beyond bodily defeat: the rulers can kill, but cannot prevent the head’s later action or control the meaning of the tree. The subsequent discourse will explicitly connect bodily remains and descendants. Claims of a particular maize-god identity, seasonal calendar or astronomical configuration require additional evidence beyond this tree episode.

## Implications for the poem — editorial possibilities

Give the head’s placement, fruiting and prohibition enough narrative space that Xquic’s curiosity has an intelligible object and risk. Preserve the difference between the older head, the calabash fruit and the younger twin’s replacement head. A future visual or poetic echo may connect them, but should be identified as composition rather than source identity.

## Outstanding research

Transcribe the transformation clause and prohibition in full. Collate *tzima* and the locality term with contemporary K’iche’ expertise. Determine which parts of the botanical explanation are Ximénez’s Spanish, Scherzer’s editing or later commentary. The precise placement of the tree in relation to the burial site is recoverable only after closer spatial-language collation; no invented map is supplied.
