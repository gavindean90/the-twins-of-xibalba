# The First Ballgame and the Fathers’ Defeat

## Narrative sequence — source evidence

The narrative first introduces the father of Hunahpu and Xbalanque, then explains the earlier family. One Hunahpu and Seven Hunahpu belong to the generation born to Xpiyacoc and Xmucane. Xbaquiyalo is One Hunahpu’s wife and mother of Hunbatz and Hunchouen; Seven Hunahpu is described as without a wife. The elder sons’ musical and craft accomplishments matter later: they are established household members, not strangers suddenly introduced as rivals. See M 12r, sequence 33; S pp. 29–30; B II.i, pp. 68–71; `pv-fathers-family`.

The men play ball; the bird Voc observes and communicates with the powers above. The noise provokes the underworld council. The lords’ purpose is hostile, though their message presents an invitation to play and entertain them. The list of officials and their harmful functions precedes the dispatch of the four owl messengers. See M 12v–13r, sequences 34–35; S pp. 30–33; `pv-fathers-summons`.

Before departure, the family is informed and the ball is left suspended in the house. The elder boys are instructed to maintain the household’s music and occupations and console their grandmother. Xmucane grieves; the departing men reassure her. This preserved equipment supplies a later causal link to the younger twins’ rediscovery of ballplaying. M 13v–14r, sequences 36–37; S pp. 33–34.

The descent is not an uninterrupted failure. The brothers negotiate water and obstacles before being deceived at the crossroads. They follow the speaking black road, greet wooden figures as rulers and sit on a hot stone seat. The lords laugh at them. In the Dark House, the visitors consume the torch and tobacco that they were required to return intact. A catalogue describes other trial houses, but the narrative expressly says these brothers did not enter them. The lords then pronounce death; the men are killed and buried, with One Hunahpu’s head separated for display in the tree. M 14r–15r, sequences 37–39; S pp. 34–37; B II.ii, especially pp. 88–89; `pv-fathers-defeat`.

## Primary-source and historical comparison

The manuscript’s left column on 15r names the brothers immediately before their appearance before Hun Came and Vucub Came, after the house catalogue. The corresponding Spanish says they did not enter those punishments. B p. 89 preserves that exclusion. It is therefore misleading to assign all the younger twins’ later trials to this first descent. The inspected scene does not narrate a completed fatal ball match after the Dark House failure; the summons is for ballplay, but the narrated route to execution is the deception and exhausted objects.

S’s habitual “infierno” belongs to Ximénez’s Christian Spanish vocabulary. B’s notes recast Xibalba as an ancient political empire and its ordeals as initiation mysteries (p. 71, note 6; pp. 88–89, note 1). These are historical interpretive frameworks, not additional events supplied by the K’iche’ narrative. B p. 69 and the preceding note also complicate the identity of the earlier brothers with racialized genealogy. Neither that account nor Sp’s characterization of One Hunahpu as a “bi-sexual personage” (`spence-txt`, lines 335–344) is adopted as a demonstrated manuscript fact.

G’s February 1906 installment, II.i–ii, follows the same family-to-summons structure. Sp compresses the Dark House sequence into sacrifice and burial, omitting the failed return of the light and tobacco. Its compression cannot establish the place of execution more precisely than the fuller account.

## Philological questions and interpretation

“Fathers” can function as a shared ancestral designation in this narrative. It must not silently erase the distinction between One Hunahpu’s wife/offspring and Seven Hunahpu’s unmarried status. Likewise, the names are kept as stable character identifiers rather than treated as independently established English etymologies. The exact morphology of the family introduction still requires K’iche’ specialist review.

The equipment, interrupted family continuity and repeated summons make the second descent intelligible. The later brothers counter specific failures of their ancestors. That patterned relation is a defensible narrative observation; a universal initiation theology or a literal historical war is not established by it.

## Implications for the poem — editorial possibilities

An expanded opening needs the household before the summons, the promise to return and the equipment left behind. Distinguish the earlier men’s partial success on the road from their failure in court. Reserve the full sequence of later houses for the younger twins. No new stanzas or fixed book divisions are proposed here.

## Outstanding research

Collate the opening family clause word by word, particularly plural and singular reference. Establish exact terminology for the equipment without importing modern game rules. Transcribe the exclusion from the later houses on 15r. Determine whether the burial-place term is best glossed as an ash place, a ballcourt expression or another reading; see the ascension dossier. Claims about both men’s decapitation remain unsupported: the head explicitly singled out is One Hunahpu’s.
